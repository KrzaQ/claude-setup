# Writing

These apply to prose a human reads later: commit messages, PR
descriptions, documentation, code comments, design notes.

- One idea per sentence, and keep sentences short. A sentence that needs
  a semicolon to hold two clauses together is usually two sentences.
- Active voice, with the actor named. "The parser drops the field", not
  "the field is dropped".
- Use the same word for the same thing throughout. Do not vary the
  wording for style — a reader has to assume a different word means a
  different thing.
- Prefer a plain verb to a noun cluster: "cache the result", not
  "result caching implementation".
- Write for a reader who is not a native speaker and was not in the
  room. Skip idiom, sarcasm, and shorthand that only makes sense today.

This is the spirit of Simplified Technical English, not the letter. Do
not try to follow ASD-STE100 literally — its approved-word list is
aerospace vocabulary and bans much of what this trade needs to say.
